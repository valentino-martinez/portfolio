A thrown pot tells you immediately when you are wrong. The wall goes
thin, the rim goes eccentric, the whole thing collapses into a wet
spiral and you start again. There is no ambiguity and no arguing.

Software almost never does this. You can be wrong for months.

## The feedback loop is the medium

What I actually value in clay is not the object at the end. It is the
tightness of the loop: hands, wheel, response, correction, all inside
a second. When I sit back down at a screen I find myself trying to
rebuild that loop artificially — shorter iterations, faster previews,
prototypes rough enough that throwing them away costs nothing.

## Tolerances

Woodworking taught me the opposite lesson. A dovetail does not
tolerate iteration. You measure, you mark, you cut once. The whole
discipline is front-loaded into thinking, and the cutting is just the
part where you find out whether the thinking was good.

Both are true. Some problems want you to move fast and feel your way
in; some want you to sit still and be certain before you touch
anything. Most of the skill is knowing which one you are holding.
